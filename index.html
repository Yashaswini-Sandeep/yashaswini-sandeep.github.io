Real work · Allianz Partners · 2026

An SLA penalty scorecard the client could trust

The problem

A premium automotive client’s roadside-assistance contract has 18 SLAs, six of which carry financial penalties. Operations and the client needed a report they could act on, and a wrong number meant a wrong penalty.

What I did

  • Catalogued all 18 SLAs and their data availability: 6 carry penalties, and 11 had no real data yet. Where data or a definition was missing, the report shows “No data” instead of a misleading value.
  • Wrote a 9-page mockup and a page-by-page Power BI build guide, then built the towing, volume forecast, CSAT, repair-on-spot and replacement-car pages, each with a written finding.
  • Verified measures line by line with DAX query view and numerator-and-denominator checks, and logged every defect with its cause and status.
  • Reconciled the report against the client’s process-mining export and billing data, including the Q3 reconciliation: the issues found were reported and fixed.
  • Added a click-through glossary overlay so business users can read each KPI definition inside the report.

What verification caught

  • Blank actuals defaulted to the worst penalty tier. I traced it through four layers of DAX to missing blank checks.
  • 249 rows with missing elapsed time were silently counted in the shortest wait bucket, and an on-time flag with no lower bound counted negative elapsed times as on time.
  • Creation and completion times were in UTC while dispatch and arrival were local, inflating waits by about two hours.
  • An outlier filter dropped 41 of 56 appointment tows, so only about 18 could be measured.

What changed

I rebuilt the appointment base without that filter. Measurable appointments rose from about 18 to about 72, and the KPI itself moved by only about a point. The gain is coverage, not a better score, and the report now says so.

My role

I built and verified the report pages and own the DAX of the monitoring pages. A colleague and I built the Databricks KPI logic and data model together, and KPI definitions come from the product owner’s SLA guide and operations sign-off. We fixed logic issues together at query level. Where a definition was missing, I escalated it.
